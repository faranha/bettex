## Key Facts

**Decision as of 3 October 2026: none of the four jurisdictions is verified as a “no approval, no own licence, no fund-recognition” home for the complete BETTEX specification. Panama is the only conditional, narrowly testable company-first candidate; it is not a go decision.** On the facts given, BETTEX takes public retail capital, commingles it in one treasury, invests it across markets, and promises holders a formula-based share of realized investment profits. Calling the claim a non-share, non-voting token does not by itself change that economic substance. Nor does deleting a redemption promise convert pooled profit-sharing investment interests into a straightforward operating-company token.

The legal options do not collapse into one “offshore crypto licence” question:

1. **Cayman Islands — high-risk mismatch with the required company-only/no-fund solution.** The Cayman private-fund definition is built around an entity issuing investment interests that pool funds from at least two investors so they may share profits/gains from acquiring, holding, managing or disposing of investments (see the contemporary legal summary, [S6], alongside the official Act sources [C1–C2]). BETTEX’s 100% public-USDC treasury and holder-weighted profit distribution map closely to those elements. Its lack of equity, votes, redemption and compulsory repurchase is not a reliable escape from the investment-interest/pooling test. It appears more like a closed-ended than redeemable fund, but “closed-ended” is not a blanket exemption: the 2025 Revision is an active statutory regime, and the 2026 amendment creates a tokenised-private-fund category and additional records, annual confirmation, disclosures, transfer-approval, reporting and supervisory powers [C1–C3]. The specific transfer-approval rule is in tension with a freely transferable token and open in-app swap unless the operator controls token transfers in the manner set out in fund documents. Cayman commentary/search evidence also identifies a possible SIBA carve-out for issuing an entity’s own securities [C4]; that is favorable only to the securities-business licensing issue and does **not** remove the separate fund characterization/registration problem. The narrow possibility that a genuinely commercial operating company falls outside “private fund” is not persuasive on this brief’s stated dominant activity: investing all holder proceeds to generate formula-based holder returns.

2. **Seychelles — not compatible without issuer registration and licensed activity.** The FSA says the Act applies to persons issuing ICOs as well as persons providing virtual-asset services in or from Seychelles; its FAQ says the issuer must register each ICO, and a publicly tradable token is not within the closed-loop exception [S1]. The FAQ also says ICO promotion is restricted to licensed VASPs or Securities Act licensees. Wallet-provider and virtual-asset exchange are licensable activity classes. A Seychelles company cannot avoid the Act just by locating its operations or customers abroad; the FSA says incorporation/registration in Seychelles itself brings the company into scope, and Seychelles IBCs must also check authorisation requirements in foreign jurisdictions [S1]. Existing transition windows ended in 2024; new applicants must obtain approval before business starts [S1–S2]. Outsourcing the app’s wallet/exchange services to an independent licensee might narrow those service issues, but it does not eliminate BETTEX’s own token-offering registration or promoter restrictions. Because the brief disallows issue approval and a proprietary licence, Seychelles is a **no**.

3. **Saint Vincent and the Grenadines (SVG) — not “license-free”; self-issue boundary unresolved, while the described app services are in the regulated lane.** The VABA took effect 31 May 2025. The FSA’s implementation circular lists virtual/fiat and virtual/virtual exchange, transfer, custody/administration, and participation in or provision of financial services related to an issuer’s offer or sale of a virtual asset as activities requiring registration [S3]. The FSA separately describes virtual-asset business as activity carried out “for or on behalf of another person” [S4]. I did not verify from the full Act whether an issuer acting only for itself is outside that definition; do not infer from the circular that every own-token issue necessarily needs registration. But BETTEX also proposes a pool/swap, app withdrawals, and retail-facing token distribution, which may involve services to holders and are not safely cleared by the own-issuer ambiguity. It may outsource genuinely independent services, but this would require real, independently licensed providers and a clean division of who controls the pool, token transfers and customer assets—not a “no-license SVG” label. Separately, SVG has an active Mutual Funds Act regime: an official 2026 Gazette search result records renewals through the end of 2026 [S5]. That is evidence against assuming the jurisdiction has no fund regulation, not enough evidence to decide whether this particular profit-participation instrument falls under the Securities Act or Mutual Funds Act. With those classification and operating-service questions unresolved, SVG is **not a defensible no-approval/no-licence choice on this record**.

4. **Panamá — conditional shortlist candidate, only if actual offer and administration stay outside Panamá and local gaming scope is resolved.** This is the only promising territorial route found, not a substantive safe harbour for a pooled investment scheme. Article 128 is described by a 2026 jurisdictional legal guide as treating an offer to persons domiciled outside Panamá as not made in Panamá [P1]. SMV material says an investment company must register when it publicly offers participation units in Panamá **or** is administered in Panamá or from Panamá [P2]. These are separate triggers. A Panamanian company that admits Panamanian-domiciled retail holders, solicits them, or makes its investment-administration decisions from Panamá could therefore move into the local securities/investment-company regime even if the token is issued from abroad. BETTEX’s broadly accessible retail token and centrally managed investment treasury make local presence and instrument characterization decisive; “no shares/no votes” is not a substantiated exemption. The SMV itself cautions that its site’s unified text is a working document, points to the Official Gazette as controlling, and warns that some base texts omit Law 67 of 2011 reforms [P3]. The exact current official-gazette provisions and BETTEX’s status as a security/investment company still need pinning down.

   **Panamá screening result: CONDITIONAL, not approved.** It is plausible to preserve a single Panamanian operating company only if (i) the actual investment administration is outside Panamá, (ii) no offer/marketing is made to Panamanian-domiciled persons, (iii) the instrument is not treated as a locally registrable investment-company participation on the actual facts, and (iv) other local activities do not require authorisation. The first two conditions are practical perimeter controls, not proof that an all-comers international retail issue qualifies. If the intended audience includes Panamanian residents or the company’s substantive treasury/AI decisions are made in Panamá, this candidate fails the current screen. This is **not** a suggestion to solve the model by excluding the four specified market types; the betting, prediction, equity/crypto, arbitrage and casino activities remain intact and create an additional gaming-perimeter question.

**Shortlist / elimination decision.** Keep **Panamá only as a conditional first screen** for a single operating company with genuinely foreign investor targeting and administration outside Panamá. Do not call this “license free,” and do not incorporate on the present record. Cayman is eliminated under the proposed no-fund constraint because the pooled-investment/profit-sharing features point strongly to a private fund and tokenised-fund rules then attach. Seychelles is eliminated because the issuer’s publicly tradable ICO itself must be registered. SVG is eliminated as a present decision because the VABA is live, the product’s app services may be in-scope, and the own-issue/securities/fund boundary was not cleared. **If BETTEX must admit the public without a Panamanian-domicile/administration perimeter and must avoid any fund/security classification or issue approval, the explicit answer is that none of the four has been shown to meet the model.**

**Entity versus fund/instrument conclusion.** A single legal entity is not necessarily one regulatory activity. The central issue is whether holders’ token is, in substance, an investment interest: holders supply capital, proceeds are pooled and put into investments, and a fixed formula allocates a share of realized portfolio profit. The specified “max/min” profit cap and 75/25 retention/distribution rule make the economic link unusually express. Lack of ownership, voting, NAV redemption or a mandatory buyback distinguishes the instrument from shares or redeemable fund units but does not negate a participation in investment returns. The private-company label and AI management mechanism do not answer the classification. Cayman supplies the clearest statutory warning; Panamá supplies a possible territorial line but no proven exemption for the instrument.

**Corporate onboarding and reputation.** No primary evidence in this research establishes that any of these four will provide BETTEX with a bank, USDC access, market counterparties, payment rails, or lower-risk corporate onboarding. Seychelles FSA’s VASP process calls for a local resident director, provisional physical-office evidence, proposed bank/financial institution, capital and risk-control evidence [S1]; that is evidence of substantive licensing friction, not an incorporation comparison. SVG’s FSA confirms an application and fee for covered virtual-asset activity [S3]. Do not infer “easy onboarding” or reputation from an incorporation agent’s marketing. Panamá’s corporate-onboarding and banking/reputation advantage, if any, remains unsubstantiated here.

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference

- **“No shares or redemption means no fund/security”: not supported.** Cayman’s pooled-capital/profit-return definition focuses on what investment interests do, and the 2026 law expressly represents a whole private-fund investment interest as a digital investment token [S6, C1–C3]. An instrument-specific opinion still needs to test the precise contractual rights and who holds the portfolio assets.
- **“Own token issuance is not regulated VASP activity”: unresolved in SVG; too broad in Seychelles.** SVG’s FSA definition refers to services “for or on behalf of another person,” but the official implementation list includes financial services related to an issuer’s offer/sale and does not resolve whether the issuer is a provider to holders [S3–S4]. Seychelles expressly places ICO issuers in scope and requires public-token ICO registration [S1].
- **“Cayman token issuance is exempt from all licensing”: false as a general claim.** A search-result legal/news trail reports an exclusion for qualifying tokenised fund interests from the VASP regime, but the 2026 Private Funds Amendment Act itself imposes fund-specific duties and does not make an unregistered, non-fund operating token immune from securities, fund or other rules [C2, C6]. It is not an issuance-approval or fund-registration bypass.
- **“Panama offers abroad are unregulated”: overbroad.** Article 128’s outside-domicile rule is a territorial scope point, not a ruling on the classification of an investment-return token, the “administered from Panama” test, the issuer’s local duties, foreign destination-country laws or gaming laws [P1–P3].
- **“The Panama crypto bill was vetoed, therefore crypto has no regulation”: not established.** The historic veto snippet concerns one bill; it does not settle later legislation, AML/reporting obligations or 2026 amendments. Current statutory status was not fully corroborated from primary law [P7].
- **“Foreign casino/sports customers are outside Panama’s gaming law”: not established.** 2026 local-news snippets report a new gambling-law reform, but they do not settle offshore issuer, remote operator, foreign-customer, or game-provider territorial coverage [P8–P9]. Polymarket predictions, stock/crypto dealing or routing, arbitrage and casino operations each need independent country-specific rules; this offshore-formation review does not declare any one of those activities legal.

## Gaps & Unanswered Questions

1. Obtain and compare the controlling, fully amended Cayman Private Funds Act, Mutual Funds Act, VASP Act as amended in 2026, and SIBA. Confirm whether an ordinary private company issuing only its own profit-linked token necessarily meets the private-fund definition and whether any relevant Cayman own-issue carve-out applies without requiring fund registration.
2. Obtain the controlling Seychelles Act/Regulations text and verify ICO registration versus any Securities Act route against the actual offer document. Current FSA FAQ already makes the public-tradable-token registration and promoter/licensed-service problem clear enough to eliminate the no-approval premise.
3. Obtain SVG’s official Securities Act, Mutual Funds Act and full VABA/2025 amendment/guidance, particularly: (a) “for or on behalf of another” applied to self-issuance, (b) whether a profit-participation contract is a security, investment interest or mutual-fund unit, (c) what “public offer” means, and (d) whether an independent provider can run the app pool/swap while the issuer retains treasury and profit allocation.
4. For Panamá, verify the current official-gazette consolidated DL 1/1999 as amended (including Article 128, definition of securities/financial instrument, public offer, investment company and administration-from-Panama tests), not merely a working text or guide. Identify whether a negotiable contract distributing realized investment gains is an instrument/participation unit and whether a Panamanian issuer can issue it to foreign retail without a local registration/recognition requirement.
5. Resolve Panama’s 2026 virtual-asset and gaming-law status from the actual promulgated texts, effective dates, implementing rules and regulator guidance; the search results do not establish foreign-customer-only treatment. Map each required market (prediction event contracts, brokerage access to equities/crypto, arbitrage, casino/promotions) to the relevant licensed counterparties and jurisdictions without dropping any market.
6. Obtain factual incorporation, registered-agent, beneficial-ownership, annual filing, local director/substance, bank/payment/USDC onboarding and reputation evidence for each finalist. No claims that a territory is “permissive” or easy to onboard should be made from promotional agent content.
7. For all four countries, distinguish the issuer’s home-country rules from countries of holders, marketing, app access, order execution, asset custody, gambling counterparties and investment management. A home-company perimeter cannot itself make a global 100,000-holder public retail offer lawful.
