## Key Facts

Research date: **3 October 2026**. Scope is the choice among Switzerland, Singapore and Hong Kong for the specified single, privately held BETTEX operating company—not a fund vehicle, a two-entity structure, or a professionally restricted issuance. The model assessed is one issuer selling a freely transferable own token to international retail; holders have contractual participation in realized company profits but no shares, votes, fractional treasury ownership, redemption or mandatory buyback. All public USDC becomes the company’s pooled treasury, invested across markets through AI; profit allocation is 75% retained/25% paid in USDC pro rata by token-time, with optional staking. For offers into Switzerland or Singapore, 100,000 retail holders are incompatible with the cited investor-count exemptions; territoriality means that does not establish the rules for foreign-only offers. The token economics also create material collective-investment-scheme risk regardless of issuer label.

### Decision

**Do not make an unconditional domicile choice or eliminate a country based solely on local public-offer rules until the offer territory is identified.** Switzerland has the clearest source in this review on own-issuance: FINMA says self-issuance of asset tokens qualifying as securities does not itself require FINMA approval. That is separate from prospectus duties for a public offer **in Switzerland** under FinSA Art. 35. A foreign issuer is not automatically within that Swiss public-offer trigger merely because it is foreign; correspondingly, Swiss issuer domicile by itself does not establish that every offer made only abroad is a Swiss public offer. A Swiss-only retail offer at the proposed scale would not fit the ordinary small-offer exemptions and would ordinarily require a reviewed prospectus. That is **prospectus review, not FINMA approval of issuance**, and not fund authorization.

The territorial distinction does not settle Switzerland’s CISA issue. Official CISO Art. 1b narrows the “operating company” exclusion to a commercial enterprise whose main purpose is managing a services, production or trading business; investments are permitted only as a subordinate/accessory activity. If BETTEX’s principal activity is investing its pooled treasury for token-holder profit participation, it likely does **not** fit this operating-company exclusion. That is a serious, substantive obstacle even for an offer only abroad, but not by itself a complete determination that every CISA element is met. Because fund authorization is disallowed as the solution, Switzerland is **not cleared**: the remaining factual/legal classification may be incompatible with the brief. CHF 20,000 fully paid-in capital for a GmbH is reported by a formation provider; it is capital, not expense. No all-in Swiss setup cost is established.

**Singapore: eliminate for the proposed public retail plan.** A public offer of the transferable profit security ordinarily requires a registered prospectus unless a narrow exemption fits. The identified exceptions—S$5 million or less in 12 months, no more than 50 offerees in 12 months, S$200,000 minimum transaction, or accredited/institutional-only—each conflicts with the stated scale, retail eligibility or accessible token denomination. More importantly, the facts appear to satisfy the central CIS indicators: participants lack day-to-day control, money is pooled and the operator invests it as a whole to enable profit participation. A licensing/offer route premised on CIS recognition would violate the brief. Calling the issuer an operating company does not displace a substance-based scheme analysis. An offering exemption for securities is not a general exemption from CIS requirements. No supported issuer-self-dealing licensing exclusion sufficient for a token-operated public distribution was established in this research.

**Hong Kong: do not eliminate solely by Companies Ordinance s. 11.** That section’s private-company restriction is expressly about public invitations to subscribe for shares or debentures and caps members at 50; the BETTEX token is not a share, and its characterization as a debenture is unresolved. If it is instead a contractual profit right/security or CIS interest, the SFO public-offer regime may independently matter when the Hong Kong public is invited. There is a territorial alternative to map: the research result on the Companies Ordinance’s prospectus definition discusses offers made outside Hong Kong to persons outside Hong Kong, and s. 103 contains its own exceptions. However, the materials retrieved here do not establish that any general “sell abroad” exception covers this token, its distribution from Hong Kong, or every SFO limb. Do not infer an exemption merely from foreign buyers or issuer domicile; equally, do not treat s. 11 as a categorical ban on every contractual token. A no-Hong-Kong-public offer scenario is legally distinct and remains open for precise statutory classification.

### Application to the fixed mechanics

* **One entity and instrument:** A separate operating issuer with a contractual, transferable profit right is analyzed as an economic security, not as a share. No profit right is made equivalent to equity solely by reference to its formula. But security classification is likely in all three jurisdictions. The lack of voting/redemption rights does not itself negate a security or CIS.
* **Treasury/investment:** The issuer’s legal title to USDC and positions supports the operating-company argument, but the offer is explicitly marketed as funded participation in centrally managed multimarket performance. “Company treasury” is not a safe harbor where proceeds and profits are pooled and managed for token-holder participation.
* **No KYC, app swaps and self-custody:** These facts do not create an issuance exemption. They make it less plausible to keep offers under investor-count / offeree limits and create separate distribution, exchange, AML, custody and sanctions questions. This research does not establish that wallet export/self-custody removes issuer, intermediary or local offer rules.
* **External strategies:** A non-subsidiary licensed provider may be able to supply market access, but that does not cure the issuer’s token/prospectus/CIS classification. No live eligibility or commercial access to Polymarket, Robinhood-era securities/crypto products, sports arbitrage or casinos was verified. Do not claim that access is available by incorporation alone.
* **Gaming exposure:** Jurisdiction of incorporation does not legalize the gaming-facing product in customer locations. Singapore’s regulator expressly treats unlicensed remote gambling offered from abroad to people in Singapore as unlawful; the Hong Kong ordinance reaches bookmaking carried on outside Hong Kong for bets placed from Hong Kong; Switzerland blocks access to unlicensed foreign online gaming sites. With no KYC today, reliable market-by-market exclusion is not demonstrated. These are customer-market constraints, not proof that every offshore BETTEX activity is prohibited.
* **Public offering thresholds are not equivalent to approval of the business:** In Switzerland, prospectus review is a disclosure-compliance gate, not FINMA authorization of a financial institution or approval/recognition of a fund. The public route remains retail-capable in principle, unlike investor-count/minimum-ticket/professional-only exemptions. However, the unclosed CISA issue means this research cannot say the reviewed prospectus alone suffices.
* **Territory matters:** FinSA Art. 35 is framed around a public offer in Switzerland. Separately assess where offer material is issued, where the public is invited, and who is targeted; foreign-only retail sales are not the same case as a Swiss public offer. SFO s. 103 and the Companies Ordinance have distinct scopes/exceptions; the search evidence does not support collapsing them into one “foreign offer” safe harbor. This is a scenario to analyze, not a recommended product restriction.

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference

1. **“Swiss public retail can be served without any approval.” — Incorrect as stated for a Swiss public offer.** FINMA’s own guidance supports no FINMA approval for self-issuing a security token, but a public offer in Switzerland remains subject to prospectus law. A prospectus review is not FINMA authorization of the issuer. Whether the offer is in Switzerland is a separate territorial question; foreign-only offers are not presumed to trigger FinSA Art. 35 solely because issuer is Swiss.
2. **“The CHF 100,000 / 500 / CHF 8 million thresholds are one combined safe harbor.” — Incorrect.** Article 36 offers alternative exemption grounds, including offer to fewer than 500 investors, consideration/denomination above CHF 100,000, and less than CHF 8 million over 12 months. The excerpted evidence is secondary and should be confirmed against the current official FinSA text and relevant calculation/aggregation rules. None is credible for a broad low-denomination 100,000-holder design at scale.
3. **“Singapore’s 50-person exemption solves a 100,000-holder plan.” — No.** It caps offers at no more than 50 persons in a 12-month period; bypassing the cap via sequential token sales or a public app has not been established as lawful. The S$5 million exemption and S$200,000 minimum are also poor fits.
4. **“One corporate issuer cannot be a CIS.” — Unsupported.** Singapore and Hong Kong CIS tests focus on how participant contributions/property are managed, control and pooling, not just legal name or corporate form. The BETTEX centralized investment and formulaic profit sharing point toward the tests, though application of the complete statutory definitions to the proprietary treasury/contractual claim needs authoritative confirmation.
5. **“Own-issue means no licensing anywhere in Hong Kong.” — Unsupported.** Even if an issuer dealing solely as principal has a relevant exclusion, it does not cover every public offer under Part IV or every intermediary service. The app’s matching, swaps, custody and public distribution must be analyzed independently.
6. **“Offshore casino/sports activity is lawful everywhere because BETTEX is incorporated offshore.” — False.** Official rules in all three jurisdictions reach at least specified services or customer-facing conduct connected to the territory. This does not establish a blanket prohibition on all foreign markets, but it does prevent domicile from serving as an answer.

## Source Quality Assessment

* **Best current primary evidence:** FINMA’s official FinTech page is direct on no FINMA approval for self-issuance and on prospectus obligations, but its cited token text is legacy guidance and it does not decide CISA status. MAS’s current offer page is clear on Singapore thresholds, but does not decide issuer licensing or the operating-company/CIS boundary. SFC’s public materials are useful for the Hong Kong securities/CIS framework; the fetched STO statement is explicitly superseded and is not treated as current controlling guidance.
* **Statutes/regulator snippets:** Official Singapore/Hong Kong statute and regulator search results provide direct but incomplete evidence on private placement, s. 103, CIS elements and gambling. The live Singapore SFA fetch was routed to a provisions view that did not include ss. 272A–275, so those offer caps are cross-referenced to MAS’s page and the separate statutory search snippet, not asserted from that fetch.
* **Secondary legal source:** Baer & Karrer and the Swissrights reproduction support the Swiss threshold summary but only at snippet level. Exact current FinSA article text, counting rules, 12-month aggregation and the reviewing body’s role require confirmation from the official consolidated law.
* **Formation costs:** ACRA’s government fee snippet is reliable. Hong Kong registry fees are from its official fee result, while the current business-registration levy and Swiss capital snapshot are supported by formation-provider snippets, not a complete official cost schedule. These are indicative and exclude registered office, corporate secretary, notarization, legal structuring, banking, tax and ongoing compliance.
* **Overall confidence:** High that Singapore’s cited domestic public-retail exemptions fail the requested scale; the Hong Kong private-company rule is not a categorical bar until the profit instrument is classified as shares/debentures, and offer territory/SFO exceptions are mapped but not resolved. CISO Art. 1b makes the Swiss operating-company exclusion unlikely if investment is BETTEX’s main activity; whether all CISA scheme elements apply remains unproven. No final country elimination should rest on issuer domicile alone. The genuine uncertainty is instrument/CIS classification and territorial application—not whether a “fund” label could solve the case (it cannot under the stated constraints).

## Gaps & Unanswered Questions

* Current official consolidated FinSA Article 35/36 and prospectus review-body rules: territory of a global online offer, targeting/advertisement, thresholds, aggregation, token transfer/secondary trading.
* CISA application to an AG/GmbH-owned multimarket treasury when token holders have only an unsecured contractual claim to realized company profit, with no redemption, no fractional beneficial ownership and no holder control. CISO Art. 1b makes the operational exclusion appear unavailable when investing is the principal activity; test all remaining CISA elements and exclusions. The entity’s Swiss domicile may leave issuer/fund regulation questions even if offers are foreign-only.
* Swiss company-law validity and tax/accounting treatment of a transferable, non-equity, no-redemption profit participation right; whether it is a bond/debt instrument, participation certificate, derivative, or other security; and any banking/deposit or financial-intermediary triggers. “No mandatory principal repayment” reduces a classic deposit/debt analogy but does not determine the result.
* Swiss AG minimum capital/paid-in requirement, formation and recurring cost from official sources, and feasibility of keeping the AG private while tokens are broadly held but do not constitute shares or membership.
* Current complete Singapore SFA provisions for security offers, issuer/principal and dealing exemptions; Securities and Futures Act CIS definition and all exclusions; licensing consequences of own-issue distribution and secondary market app functions.
* Current Hong Kong SFO Schedule 1 CIS definition and all limbs/exclusions, Companies Ordinance restrictions as applied to the exact profit-right contract, prospectus/private-offer exceptions, current 2023+ tokenized-securities guidance, and boundaries of an issuer’s own-account exemption.
* Exact application of the Companies Ordinance prospectus exclusion for offers made outside Hong Kong to persons outside Hong Kong and the distinct SFO s. 103 exceptions to BETTEX’s instrument/distribution. Determine whether marketing or issuance from Hong Kong reaches Hong Kong’s public even where buyers are offshore.
* Market-by-market assessment of bets, prediction markets, sports arbitrage, casino bonuses, and USDC settlements—including geolocation and marketing rules—and whether licensed independent providers can serve the intended countries. Incorporation does not itself create market access.
