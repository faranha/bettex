## Key Facts

**Decision (as of 3 October 2026): NO-GO for constituting BETTEX in El Salvador or Serbia under the stated no-approval/no-license constraint. Dubai is the only conditional candidate among these three, but is not presently a demonstrated “yes” for the intact model.** The available evidence does not establish a jurisdiction where BETTEX can issue this profit-bearing public token to international retail without either a materially relevant approval/regulated distribution pathway or a still-unresolved financial-product classification. This is a comparative elimination, not a recommendation to obtain an issuance/fund approval.

Assumptions applied: BETTEX is one privately held operating company; the public token is transferable and exchange-tradable, does not represent shares/votes, treasury units, redemption or mandatory buyback; public USDC becomes company treasury and is invested using management discretion across strategies; token holders have a non-discretionary claim on 25% of qualifying realized, retained-earnings/NAV-limited profits, measured by token-weight-time; up to 100,000 retail holders are sought in year one; and the present onboarding has no KYC. I have not assumed a particular offering amount where the prompt gives none. If “20m” means a €20 million issue, it is used below only as a conditional comparison to statutory caps, not as an established target amount.

### 1. Dubai / UAE — conditional candidate, not a confirmed viable incorporation

VARA’s current issuance framework has three categories. **Category 1** includes fiat-referenced assets, asset-referenced virtual assets (ARVAs), and other types VARA designates; it requires a VARA issuance licence before issuance. **Category 2** is the residual class: it needs no issuer licence or prior VARA approval, but **all placement and distribution must be through a VARA-licensed distributor**. Exempt VAs are limited to non-transferable or redeemable closed-loop assets (and any further type VARA might later designate), and may not enable markets to form. A publicly tradable BETTEX token is therefore not the described current exemption. Issuers in Categories 1 and 2 generally publish a whitepaper and separate risk disclosure; publication is not itself regulatory approval. The April 9, 2026 guidance expressly says it is indicative, not a substitute for the regulations.

The guidance’s concrete profit-token example matters but is **not a conclusive match**: its example RE1 provides rental income and a share of profits from sales of a Dubai real-estate portfolio and is Category 1 because the entitlement derives from tangible real-world assets. BETTEX’s reward is linked to company-wide, multimarket profits (realized income capped by NAV/base), not a specified real-estate asset, and BETTEX has no redemption or ownership rights. The facts therefore do not prove ARVA or Category 1 status; equally, the share-like economic participation makes it unsafe to assume Category 2. VARA retains discretion to classify according to the represented rights, value and underlying business model. The Dubai/Federal Capital Market Authority securities perimeter is also relevant to a contractually enforceable profit claim. Current official sources establish a new federal virtual-assets regime but do not classify this particular profit right as a security or an ARVA. **The classification is genuinely unresolved, not evidence that approval is certainly required.**

Category 2 would still contradict a model that insists on no licensed intermediary in token placement/distribution. The founder permits an independent, non-subsidiary provider, so this alone does not eliminate Dubai if such a distributor could support the offer; however, the distributor cannot be replaced by simply calling the token “exempt.” The app’s pool/swap, transfer and withdrawal functions also require activity-by-activity analysis: if the single company is itself a VARA-licensed VASP, VARA says that its VASP licence cannot be used for proprietary trading or trading its group portfolio and that a separate company must do that trading. That is a direct conflict with the one-company rule **if** BETTEX itself needs that VASP licence. It is not established that outsourcing the user-facing services to independent providers leaves BETTEX outside VASP status.

VARA’s official activity page says VA proprietary trading requires a VARA no-objection certificate and that trading above specified volume must be registered. A 20 January 2026 legal commentary search-result snippet reports a USD 250 million registration threshold for a “significant proprietary investor.” The threshold metric was not visible in the official material fetched, so this figure is **secondary/snippet-only**, not a sufficiently verified budget or safe harbour. It is a conditional constraint, not proof that a smaller BETTEX treasury can trade without any regulatory classification.

GCGRA is the sole UAE authority for licences to conduct commercial gaming in the UAE, and its official licensing page says those wishing to operate or facilitate gaming must be licensed. It does not address an offshore corporate entity acting only as a customer of a separately licensed bookmaker/casino, nor does it decide whether placing the company’s own wagers abroad amounts to UAE commercial gaming. Therefore “corporate offshore client” is not evidenced as automatically prohibited or as automatically permitted. GCGRA is not by itself a basis to reject Dubai incorporation; nor can a permitted customer relationship be assumed. No official price or decision-time for the specific BETTEX path was established.

**Applied result: CONDITIONAL / not yet suitable as a positive choice.** Dubai is the only one of these three where the current issuance rules expressly contemplate a residual category without issuer licence or prior approval. That route works only if VARA/CMA classification puts this profit-linked token outside Category 1/securities, all placement/distribution is handled by an independent licensed distributor, the company is not forced into a second-company split for proprietary trading, and present no-KYC onboarding can be maintained lawfully. None of those conditions is proven on the record. Do not represent Dubai as “exempt,” and do not launch on that premise.

### 2. El Salvador — eliminate: CNAD public-offer authorization conflicts with stated constraint

The LEAD applies to public digital-asset offers **in El Salvador** and identifies a foreign issuer if it targets potential Salvadoran purchasers (subject to an unsolicited, buyer-initiated relationship exception); it also makes an entity domiciled in El Salvador an issuer. Its definition of a public **income offer** expressly includes a purchaser’s right to profits, income or distributions of returns in digital or fiat assets. BETTEX’s 25% token-holder profit distribution fits that statutory type on the stated facts, regardless of the no-share/no-redemption label.

The Act requires every public offer to be “habilitada” by CNAD. An issuer must supply the relevant information document (DIR) and the certifier’s report; an authorized certifier reviews the requirements before the Commission’s public-offer authorization. The Act provides that issuer registration follows authorization of the offer, so merely registering the company/token is not an alternative to authorization. A 2026 CNAD public notice for a revenue token illustrates current treatment, but does not constitute authority that a different offer will be approved. The statute sets an issuer public-offer fee of 0.01% of the enabled offer amount plus US$50 for a certified authorization resolution (subject to the statutory exceptions); these are statutory fees, not total legal/compliance cost. No official completion period for BETTEX’s circumstances is evidenced here.

The statute does not prove that foreign customers everywhere trigger Salvadoran law solely because the company is incorporated there: territorial public-offer scope and any foreign-platform facts still matter. But a Salvadoran-domiciled single issuer that publicly issues its own income token cannot rely on the foreign-offer carveout to avoid the issuer/offer regime. Because CNAD enabling/authorization is an unacceptable condition in the request, **incorporation in El Salvador is a NO-GO**, not a solution by calling the instrument a digital asset rather than a security.

### 3. Serbia — eliminate: no-public-approval exceptions are far below public retail scale; separate AIF/securities and gambling paths compound the mismatch

The Serbian Digital Assets Act calls a digital token an intangible property right represented digitally; its definition can include an issuer claim or a right to a share in profit. It divides supervision between NBS (virtual currencies) and the Securities Commission (digital tokens and digital assets qualifying as financial instruments).

For an initial offer, Article 16 prohibits advertising without an approved white paper unless an exception applies. Article 17 permits advertising without approved white paper only in any one of the following cases: offer addressed to **fewer than 20 persons**; no more than **20 tokens** issued; each buyer invests at least **€50,000**; or the issuer’s total 12-month issuance does not exceed **€100,000**. These are alternative exceptions, not a general “20 buyers / €100,000 public offering” allowance. A widely marketed retail token targeting 100,000 holders cannot use the fewer-than-20-person exception; a €20 million annual offer, if that is the target, cannot use the €100,000 exception. Nor can BETTEX plausibly use the €50,000-per-buyer route for mass retail. The no-approved-white-paper exception is thus unavailable at the stated scale.

The Article 7 threshold is a **different** rule: where a token has all features of a financial instrument, capital-market law ordinarily applies. Only for a non-share, non-share-fungible token with no more than €3 million issued by one issuer in 12 months does that capital-market-law treatment not apply. If the BETTEX claim is a financial instrument, this narrow €3 million clause cannot cover an issue above that cap; Article 17’s unrelated €100,000 advertising exception does not enlarge it. Whether BETTEX’s profit claim satisfies the financial-instrument test is a classification question, but the clear outcome either way is that a 100,000-person public offer has no small-offer route without an approved white paper.

There is an additional **fund** risk, separate from digital-asset issuance. BETTEX solicits pooled public money, invests it by a common strategy at the company’s discretion, and distributes investment-linked gains to holders. Those facts resemble collective investment, though I cannot conclude from the evidence that a single operating company with token creditors is necessarily an AIF. Serbia’s AIF and Capital Markets laws were amended by Official Gazette 109/2025, effective 12 December 2025. A recent Serbian law-firm explanation says retail AIF offers remain subject to Securities Commission prior approval and added subscription, redemption/payment and disclosure protections, including for third-country offers. If the AIF regime applies, that approval path independently violates the instruction not to solve the model by fund recognition/approval. The 2019 English AIF translation is not a current consolidated text; I do not rely on historical fee/capital figures.

Games of Chance Act Articles 10 and 122 prohibit enabling participation in electronic games organized by an entity without the requisite Serbian approval and allow access/payment blocking; the Act prescribes approval, capital/security, server and player-age checks for licensed electronic operators. That is a serious barrier if BETTEX acts as an unlicensed bookmaker/casino operator or offers bets to users in Serbia. It does **not**, on the reviewed text, establish that a foreign-incorporated corporate customer placing its own wagers with licensed foreign venues must itself hold a Serbian operator licence. The company-as-corporate-client scenario remains unresolved and is not needed to reach the no-go based on public token issue/AIF requirements. Similarly, one cannot infer that all foreign sports-arbitrage or market positions are games organised in Serbia.

**Applied result: NO-GO under stated constraints.** The statutory small-offer routes cannot serve the public 100,000-holder issuance, and BETTEX forbids the necessary approved-white-paper route. The distinct financial-instrument cap and plausible retail-AIF classification make treating Serbia as an operational-company workaround particularly unsound. Serbia would become discussable only if the founders accepted the very approvals the brief rules out or changed the scale/instrument; neither is an available recommendation here.

### Cross-jurisdiction choice

Among Dubai, El Salvador and Serbia, the evidence supports this order:

1. **Eliminate El Salvador**: the income-token public offer needs CNAD habilitation/authorization and issuer registration follows it.
2. **Eliminate Serbia**: the under-20 / €100,000 / €50,000-per-buyer exceptions do not cover mass public retail; an approved white paper is required, and AIF/securities classification adds approval risk.
3. **Keep Dubai only as a conditional research candidate**, not as an approved formation decision: a Category 2 issuance may avoid issuer licensing/VARA prior approval but requires a licensed distributor, while BETTEX’s profit entitlement, potential securities/AIF status, own-treasury activity and possible VASP status remain unresolved. Current no-KYC and one-company assumptions have not been shown compatible.

Thus **none of the three is a proven “yes” for the entire unchanged model**. If choosing only from these alternatives today, Dubai is the provisional best candidate for diligence, but not an instruction to incorporate or launch. A categorical Dubai recommendation would overstate evidence; the remaining uncertainty is substantive classification and perimeter, not a generic “consult counsel” disclaimer.

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference

- **“BETTEX is exempt in Dubai because it is not a stablecoin.”** Incorrectly incomplete. Category 1 also covers ARVAs and any additional VA type VARA designates; Category 2 still requires licensed distribution; the profit/rent example is Category 1; transferability and market formation prevent reliance on the current exempt categories.
- **“A token without shares or redemption is not a security / fund.”** Not established. Profit rights can be financial claims without voting equity or redemption. The federal securities and AIF classification depends on substance, rights, marketing and pooled investment facts.
- **“Serbia permits 20 retail buyers and €100,000.”** Misstates separate Article 17 exceptions. The 20-person case is fewer than 20 addressees; €100,000 is an independent 12-month total issue limit; another independent case requires at least €50,000 from each purchaser. Article 7’s separate financial-instrument limit is €3m and has its own non-share/non-fungibility tests.
- **“El Salvador only requires issuer registration.”** Inaccurate for public offers: CNAD enables/authorizes the offering; registration follows that authorization. A token conferring profits is expressly an income offer.
- **“GCGRA requires every offshore corporate gambler to be licensed.”** Not proved by the official page reviewed; it speaks to conducting/facilitating commercial gaming in the UAE and does not explain offshore corporate customers’ proprietary accounts.
- **“Dubai’s $250m registration threshold is a verified safe harbour.”** Not proved by the fetched primary text. The figure appeared in a secondary legal commentary snippet; the official VARA page confirms NOC and registration above volume thresholds but not the metric/number in its accessible text.
- **“Serbia’s AIF regime certainly captures BETTEX.”** Also not proved. The pooled discretionary investment and profit distribution strongly raise the question; classification requires the updated operative AIF text and BETTEX-specific facts. The conditional risk is enough to reject “fund recognition solves it,” not to state a conclusive regulator determination.

- Primary text and precise denominator/measurement period for VARA’s proprietary trading registration threshold; whether the reported USD 250m is assets under management, annual turnover, portfolio size or another trigger, and whether the new federal CMA framework changes it.
- Whether SCA/CMA treats the BETTEX profit right as a security, fund interest or another instrument and whether such treatment triggers issuer/intermediary licensing or investor limits in Dubai.
- Whether Serbia’s current consolidated AIF Act covers a single commercial operating company that takes token proceeds into its own treasury and distributes contractual earnings, and the consequences under the 2025 amendment.
- Whether the company’s corporate wager account can be accepted by particular GCGRA-licensed or foreign gaming operators, and whether treasury-owned gambling positions create a separate UAE operating/facilitation issue.
- Cross-border rules of each target market remain outside this three-jurisdiction screen; incorporation does not exempt BETTEX from distribution, gambling, securities, AML/KYC or consumer laws where retail holders are located.
- No official all-in incorporation/licensing costs or approval timelines were found for the described fact pattern. El Salvador’s LEAD public-offer statutory fee is the only specific statutory amount noted above, not an all-in estimate.
