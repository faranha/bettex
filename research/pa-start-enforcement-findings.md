# Panamá — start of activity before the required licence and effect on later approval

**Cutoff: 3 October 2026.** This is a targeted enforcement / founder-eligibility gap review for the described BETTEX plan: one private company, publicly offered transferable non-equity token, pooled USDC treasury investing across securities, crypto, prediction markets, sports and casino strategies, with token-time allocation of a contractually formula-based share of profits. It does not classify BETTEX conclusively or determine which of its activities in fact require a Panama authorization.

## Key Facts

- **The central question cannot be answered “yes, prior operation automatically bars later approval” on the sources located.** No retrieved primary source states a universal, automatic, permanent disqualification from a later licence merely because the company previously conducted an activity without that licence. Conversely, no evidence establishes that operating first is harmless, automatically curable, or that a later licence retroactively legalizes the earlier period. A future licence is not a substitute for authorization due before commencement.

- **For Panama-facing online gaming, the best located rule is expressly ex ante.** An official MEF-hosted 2020 JCJ resolution's search excerpt says no natural or legal person may advertise or operate internet games of chance or wager-generating activities in Panama without prior authorization through a JCJ Contract of Administration and Operation; a licence from another jurisdiction does not authorize the activity in Panama. An attempted full fetch was rate-limited. The excerpt does not settle whether BETTEX, when betting only for its own account with an external venue, is an operator or advertiser within the rule. This is not a basis to characterize every BETTEX wager as an offence.

- **The 2026 gaming reform raises enforcement risk but remains incompletely verified here.** Search-result reports attribute to Law 527 of 23 May 2026 powers to block or restrict unauthorised betting sites and fines potentially up to US$1 million. The enacted statute itself was not fetched or examined; a trade-press fetch returned 404, and another official MEF PDF fetch returned 429. Thus those reported powers are a warning, not a verified statement of the current law's exact scope, procedure, affected persons, fine trigger, or whether later licensure cures anything.

- **The securities-law material shows specific, not universal, eligibility rules.** The SMV-hosted working consolidation's Article 79 sets out enumerated restrictions, including five-year consequences related to revocation of certain specified licences/authorizations; Article 52 allows licence suspension/revocation in identified situations such as materially false or misleading application disclosures or failure to meet requirements. These are not the same as an automatic bar triggered by any earlier unlicensed conduct. Nor do they establish that no wider honesty, suitability, fit-and-proper, sanctions, criminal, or discretionary test applies. The consolidation is expressly not an official publication; the authoritative Gazette and later amendments need review.

- **Severe criminal consequences are activity-specific.** An official Superintendencia de Bancos notice fetched for this review says unauthorised operation of a *Casa de Remesas de Dinero* is an offence under Penal Code Article 253-A, carrying five to eight years' imprisonment and applying to natural and legal persons. That illustrates that some regulated activities have express criminal penalties; it does not make BETTEX a remittance business or import the remittance penalty into securities, investment-company, gaming, or token activity.

- **No sourced path to retrospective “regularization” or a provisional start was established.** The review found no verified BETTEX-relevant sandbox, grace period, provisional licence or statutory amnesty that would permit beginning before authorization. Absence of search evidence is not proof that no sector-specific arrangement exists; it is not permission to launch. Obtain the actual law/rules and written agency/counsel advice before relying on any transition mechanism.

- **Do not exclude BETTEX merely because an earlier application was refused.** This task does not treat a prior refusal, standing alone, as a permanent disqualification. The located securities text ties some bars to particular revoked licences and identified facts, and separately penalizes false application material or unmet requirements; it does not show that a refusal itself automatically prohibits every later application. The reason for any refusal, any order to cease, alleged misrepresentation, enforcement finding, conviction, or sanctions record could change the analysis. Disclose relevant history truthfully and assess the exact statute, decision, applicant and licence.

- **The right operational flow depends on what actually needs permission—not simply on whether a company can be formed.** If the proposed token/public pool or investment administration is an SMV-regulated security/investment-company activity in or from Panama, the defensible sequence is: (1) assess/classify before public offer or handling investor money; (2) obtain the required authorization/licence and any offering/registration approvals; (3) commence only once effective, while observing continuing conditions. If local online gaming operation/advertising is involved, obtain the required JCJ authorization before that operation. Activities independently determined not to be regulated may start without that specific licence, subject to all other laws; this research does not establish that BETTEX is outside the perimeter.

- **Practical effect of the founder's “any later licence” change:** it may preserve an entity-first strategy and a later application route; it does **not** justify launching the regulated offer or operations while the application is pending. Formation and internal non-public preparation are distinct from public solicitation, accepting pooled USDC, issuing profit rights, investment management, or operating/advertising gaming. Those actions should not be bundled into an assumed licence-free pre-launch phase.

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference

- **“If an authorization can be obtained later, we can operate first.”** Not established. Prior authorization language exists for online gaming advertised/operated in Panama; no source supplies a general grace period or retrospective cure.
- **“Every pre-licence period automatically bars a later licence.”** Not established. A universal automatic exclusion was not found; this is not a positive eligibility opinion and does not address sector-specific disqualification or regulator discretion.
- **“An old application refusal permanently disqualifies the founder/company.”** Not established. The retrieved law instead lists particular disqualifications and consequences tied to specified roles, revocations, convictions and application conduct. The reason and terms of any actual refusal must be checked.
- **“No KYC/private entity means no enforcement.”** Unsupported. Form of entity or absent KYC does not answer whether a public offer, investment-company management or gaming operation is regulated.
- **“The 2026 Law 527 definitely authorizes immediate blocking and a US$1m BETTEX fine.”** Not verified against the enacted text; search-result reports require cross-checking.
- **“The remittance statute proves BETTEX operators face five to eight years.”** False analogy. The cited notice is limited to remittance-house operation.
- **“No sandbox was found, therefore a sandbox is legally impossible.”** Also unsupported. No verified BETTEX-relevant sandbox / grace mechanism was found in the limited search; ask the correct regulator and review implementing regulations.
- **“Anything done before a licence is granted can later be legalized.”** No source supports this. A subsequent approval generally answers prospective permission only unless the relevant legislation or express regulator decision says otherwise.

## Gaps & Unanswered Questions

1. Obtain official Gazette No. 26979-A and all amendments in force on 2026-10-03; identify the current sanctions, offer/issuer/investment-company authorization, and natural-person/company eligibility tests. Determine whether any offence or administrative finding for operating without authorization triggers a particular bar, a suitability assessment, or regulator discretion.
2. Obtain the full current JCJ online-gaming rules and Law 527 of 23 May 2026 with implementing regulations. Confirm commencement, any transition/grace provisions, cease/block/fine/criminal powers, personal/corporate liability, appeal and cure/regularization effects.
3. Obtain an SMV/JCJ or qualified Panama counsel determination of the precise proposed conduct: accepting USDC from the public, issuing an instrument with a company-profit claim, managing pooled treasury, making sports/casino wagers for the company's own account, prediction-market activity, and any marketing/platform function. Distinguish own-account customer from operator/intermediary.
4. Establish whether any prior authorization request was refused and obtain the actual decision and reasons. Determine whether it was an application refusal, licence revocation, order to cease, adverse finding, or only informal advice. Do not assume either a permanent bar or irrelevance.
5. Clarify which founder, directors, officers, beneficial owners and company apply for each permission, and which individual fit-and-proper/conviction/sanctions tests govern each one.
6. Ask whether a specific pre-application consultation, regulatory sandbox, written no-action position, or transition procedure is available. Do not begin any activity that needs authorization unless a statute or written regulator measure affirmatively permits it.
7. Separately analyze what company formation, internal system testing, non-public treasury funding, public marketing, token issuance, user deposits and actual trading constitute; a bare corporation may be formed before a licence where allowed, but that does not authorize regulated business.
