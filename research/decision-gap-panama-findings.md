## Key Facts

**Applied decision as of 3 October 2026: PANAMA is a CONDITIONAL, best-supported company-first candidate for local-scope purposes—not a cleared or unconditional formation choice.** Panama has a formal territorial rule: under Securities Market Law Article 128, an offer to people domiciled outside Panama is not treated as made in Panama merely because it originates there. The 2026 Chambers guide also states the converse: an offer to people domiciled in Panama is treated as made there regardless of where it originates. The SMV's investment-company material identifies a separate registration trigger where participation units are publicly offered in Panama **or** the investment company is administered in/from Panama. A Panamanian issuer can therefore fall outside those local offer/administration triggers if, in fact, all relevant purchasers are non-Panamanian and investment decisions/administration are genuinely outside Panama. This is a territorial statutory perimeter, not an issuer or token exemption.

**Formal local exemption question: YES, conditionally, as to a foreign-domiciled public offer and Panama investment-company registration.** It is not enough to incorporate a Panamanian company and declare management abroad. BETTEX must substantively keep offer recipients outside Panama, not make the relevant investment-management decisions from Panama, and avoid other regulated activity performed in or from Panama. The 2026 guide says Panamanian incorporation or receipt of administrative services alone does not necessarily make a fund managed from Panama, but effective decision-making and other indicia of presence matter. This conclusion depends on a current-law cross-check: the fetched statutory consolidation is an SMV working document, not the controlling Gazette publication; the SMV says its law materials were updated through January 2022 and identifies the Official Gazette as controlling. The 2026 Chambers treatment corroborates Article 128, but is secondary law guidance.

**BETTEX's current unrestricted, no-KYC public-access configuration does not establish those conditions.** With no KYC or other reliable residency control, BETTEX cannot demonstrate that no Panama-domiciled retail buyer is solicited or admitted. An international public offer is not automatically an offer only to non-residents. If Panama-domiciled users can subscribe, or if treasury/AI decisions or directors effectively manage the investment company from Panama, the local trigger may apply. That would be incompatible with the no-approval/no-fund-recognition constraint. A genuine foreign-only distribution boundary and actual foreign management could preserve the Article 128 route without changing the single-company structure or removing any investment strategy, but it does not exempt destination countries' laws.

**The instrument remains a material, unresolved classification issue—not a proven local prohibition and not a crypto-token safe harbour.** BETTEX takes public USDC, pools it in company treasury for investment, and grants a transferable token a formula-based contractual share in 25% of qualifying realized profits. It is not a share, has no vote or redemption, and does not promise treasury ownership or buyback. Panama's investment-company definition/material concerns participation units sold to obtain public funds for investment/trading; the retrieved statutory text also defines public offers and securities broadly. Whether a negotiable, non-equity contractual profit claim is a “participation unit” or another security within the amended Panamanian law is not resolved by the sources. The SMV's 2025 Opinion 04-2025 is particularly useful but does **not** decide this: it evaluates a non-custodial BTC/ETH exchange platform and distinguishes those assets from financial instruments partly because they do not carry rights to cash flows or dividends tied to an entity's economic performance. BETTEX's explicit company-profit entitlement is materially different. This is a reason not to analogize BETTEX to Bitcoin, not a direct SMV ruling that BETTEX is a security or fund.

**Entity-versus-fund conclusion:** keeping one private commercial company is legally distinct from whether its instrument or dominant activity is treated as an investment-company/fund interest. Foreign targeting and foreign administration may remove the Panama-local offer/management trigger; they do not transform pooled, formula-profit participation into an ordinary utility token, and they do not eliminate any foreign fund/securities rules. No evidence supports using fund recognition/approval as a solution, and this review does not recommend it.

**Games: distinguish operator from customer.** The located historical statutory text says games of chance and activities generating wagers performed in Panama must be authorized/regulated; regulator-facing materials concern administrators/operators. The retrieved 2026 press reports describe broader gambling/platform oversight. I found no evidence in those sources of a separate Panama operator licence imposed merely because a foreign company is a customer wagering for its own account with a foreign operator. But I could not verify the enacted 2026 amendment's complete primary text or an express own-account/nonresident customer carve-out. Thus “the 2026 reform does not license foreign customers” is **not proved**, while “Panama necessarily licenses every foreign corporate customer” is also unsupported. The distinction cannot be used to clear BETTEX's company-owned casino, sports arbitrage or prediction positions; whether its conduct is only proprietary wagering or amounts to organizing/facilitating a game remains fact- and territory-dependent.

**Choice among entity and fund route:** retain Panama as the leading conditional incorporation jurisdiction for further decision-making, because Article 128 supplies an actual nonresident-offer perimeter and the guide/materials distinguish offshore management from local management. Do not choose it as a “yes” for the unchanged app today: the current no-KYC/global-access model cannot evidence the core residency condition; the instrument's profit right has no direct official classification; gaming-law 2026 text and actual account eligibility remain open. If the model must be available without residency controls to Panama buyers and must avoid every approval or investment-company/fund classification, **Panama is a NO under that premise and no positive choice is established by this research**. If an offer truly stays with non-Panama domiciled buyers and actual investment administration stays abroad, Panama is a **conditional local-scope YES**, not a globally compliant answer. This finding does not assume professional-only sales, removal of any requested markets, a subsidiary, a new licence, or fund approval.

Other necessary boundaries: Article 128 is about where an offer is deemed made, not about a token's substantive status, foreign investor laws, tax, AML/KYC, or access to Polymarket, Robinhood, sportsbooks, casinos or execution venues. SMV Opinion 04-2025 addresses a non-custodial exchange model and does not establish the legality of BETTEX's app pool/swap, its treasury trading or offer. No source reviewed confirms that Robinhood accepts BETTEX as a corporate account or that named prediction/gaming providers will accept a corporate own-account customer. External independent counterparties may be relevant, but availability and licensing were not established here.

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference

- **“A Panama company can sell BETTEX anywhere without SMV registration.”** Too broad. Article 128 applies to offers to non-Panama-domiciled persons; an offer to a Panama-domiciled person is treated as made in Panama. Separate local administration and activity triggers remain, and destination-country law is untouched.
- **“Article 128 is a discretionary offshore exemption or token exemption.”** No. It is a territorial rule, not an SMV ruling that this token is not a security/investment-company interest. Its concrete application depends on who is domiciled where and where the effective decision-making occurs.
- **“Panamanian incorporation alone means administration from Panama.”** Not supported by the 2026 guide: incorporation or administrative services alone are not enough if effective investment decisions are not controlled there. Conversely, nominal offshore directors do not prove the management is abroad if actual decisions, control, domicile or marketing establish Panama presence.
- **“A token without shares, votes or redemption cannot be an investment interest.”** Not established. The SMV's 2025 no-cash-flow crypto discussion concerns BTC/ETH; it expressly contrasts financial instruments carrying cash-flow/dividend rights tied to an entity's performance. BETTEX's express profit claim is different, though the opinion did not classify a BETTEX-like issuer token.
- **“Panama's SMV already said profit tokens are securities.”** Not found. Opinion 04-2025 is about a non-custodial crypto-exchange/custody business and is not an own-profit-token opinion. Exact classification remains genuinely unresolved.
- **“A foreign company placing its own wagers needs a Panama gaming-operator licence.”** Not established by evidence reviewed; operator obligations and a customer placing its own bet are distinct. Equally, no verified primary 2026 text establishes a blanket exemption for foreign corporate customers.
- **“The 2026 gaming law definitely does not apply to a foreign own-account bettor.”** Also not established: the enacted 2026 statute and its territorial/customer provisions were not captured in full primary text. Do not turn absence of a found customer-licence requirement into a positive authorization.
- **“Outside Panama means no financial regulation anywhere.”** False. Article 128 only answers the Panama securities-offer situs question. Management abroad, retail buyers, trading/execution, custody, gaming and app services can trigger laws in other places.

## Source Quality Assessment

- **Best primary legal evidence:** the SMV-hosted consolidated Securities Market Law text, which supplies Article 128, the investment-company and security/public-offer definitions, and management tests. It is a regulator-prepared working text rather than official Gazette publication. The SMV's own law index warns that it is not official, points to Gazette No. 26979-A, says materials are updated through January 2022, and lists later changes. It must not be represented as a current 2026 official consolidation.
- **Best current legal cross-reference:** Chambers' 2026 Panama guide, fetched in full, directly confirms Article 128's nonresident offer rule, its converse for Panama-domiciled recipients, and distinguishes Panama incorporation from effective management. This is a legal-practice guide (Tier 2), not statutory text or a binding regulator opinion.
- **SMV official interpretive evidence:** Opinion 04-2025 was fetched as the complete attached opinion (not only its landing page). It is an official, fact-specific opinion about a non-custodial BTC/ETH platform. Its distinction between crypto without issuer cash-flow rights and a financial instrument with cash-flow/dividend rights is relevant, but it does not directly classify an issuer's own profit token.
- **Investment-company evidence:** an SMV-hosted educational PDF states that registration applies to public offers of participation units in Panama or administration in/from Panama. It is useful for the trigger, but not a substitute for the amended law. The formal Article 128 interpretation was cross-checked against the fetched Chambers 2026 guide.
- **Gaming evidence is materially weaker:** an official historical Gazette snippet gives a territorial rule for games/wagers performed in Panama, while 2026 reform coverage is Tier 3 press material. The search did not capture an enacted Law 403/527 primary text or a specific ruling about a foreign company betting for its own account. No definitive customer exemption is claimed.
- **Search-only materials:** captured with title, URL, date, tier and quality at `research/sources/decision-gap-panama-snippets.md`. The SMV's cryptocurrency warning landing page did not expose substantive text; no legal conclusion is based on it. Opinion 04-2025 was separately fetched in full.
