# Gap fill — scale, unique bettors and survey universes

**Research cutoff:** 2026-10-03  
**Collection:** 2026-10-03. Primary-page fetches: SPA's 2025 panorama PDF and ANBIMA's 9th-edition page. See the linked full-page extractions below.  
**Purpose:** Distinguish unique people in a defined period from cumulative CPF records, active accounts, survey estimates and GGR. Do not call 100,775,427 “people.”

## Facts

- **SPA's strongest official full-year 2025 figure located:** 25.2 million Brazilians made bets during 2025, reported by the 79 authorized companies. The Ministry of Finance release dated 2026-01-23 calls this a year-of-activity total, not registrations. This is the best available official annual scale figure in this search. SPA's release does not publish a 26.4 million annual count.
- **Official SPA first-half 2025 figure:** 17.7 million Brazilians placed fixed-odds bets with authorized sites/apps, per the Finance Ministry's 2025-08-26 release. This is a half-year activity count; do not add it to the annual 25.2 million.
- **SPA Jan-2026 presentation:** covers data reported by operators via SIGAP for 2025-01-01 through 2025-12-30; it lists R$36,959,783,379.70 as GGR. GGR is not total stakes, deposits, bettor income, losses, or an investment-market TAM. The extracted presentation places 100,775,427 alongside headings for unique CPFs and active accounts at operators/brands and marks the data “acumulados.” Whether read as a CPF stock or platform-account stock, that accumulated administrative figure is not an annual 2025 unique-person count. Do not present it as 100 million current/annual bettors; use the SPA's separately stated 25.2 million full-year activity figure for 2025.
- **No primary-source confirmation found for 26.4 million unique people in 2025**, nor for a full-year number measured as unique natural persons with explicit deduplication rules. The SPA's 25.2 million release is not interchangeable with the presentation's cumulative figure.
- **First half 2026:** an article published 2026-09-25 reports 30.4 million people who bet in the first half, alongside 31.82 million CPFs with active status in government registration by September. It is secondary reporting and the two figures describe different concepts and periods. An official SPA first-half 2026 primary release/report confirming 30.4 million was not located; do not promote this to an authoritative count or conflate it with registrations.
- **DataSenado 2024 measures a recent self-reported window, not annual bettors:** its probability phone survey interviewed 21,808 people from 2024-06-05 to 2024-06-28, representing 169,840,184 Brazilians aged 16+. The report's four reported spend brackets for the prior 30 days total 20,344,587 estimated people (about 12% of that universe). The Senate's 2024-10-01 news release instead summarizes 13% / 22.13 million in the last 30 days. These same-study figures conflict; the primary report's bracket sum and the release's rounded headline are not two separate populations. Preserve the discrepancy and do not choose/reconcile it without a source explanation.
- **ANBIMA/Datafolha 9th Raio X (2026 report, behavior in 2025):** fieldwork ran 2025-11-04–21, with 5,832 in-person interviews representing people aged 16+ (estimated universe 168.1 million). The survey says 17% made online bets in 2025; 39% of bettors cited quick money when needed as their motivation; 32% cited fun; 20% saw betting as an investment. It reports average **household** income of R$5,402/month among bettors, not individual income. These are survey estimates/self-reports, not operator-verified accounts or evidence of investment intent, ability to pay, willingness to buy, or likely conversion.
- **ANBIMA's separately described qualitative bets study:** the association's 2026-09-02 news item says 20% of bettors regard bets as a kind of investment and 17% of the economically active population bet online in 2025. Treat qualitative participant narratives as qualitative, not a nationally representative prevalence estimate; do not merge the 20% with Raio X metrics unless the underlying study and bases are the same.

## Gaps / cautions

1. Obtain or visually inspect the full SPA slide/chart and data dictionary to establish exactly which column contains 100,775,427, whether it is an account/registration or unique-CPF stock, the accumulation start date, and the deduplication rules.
2. No official SPA 2026-H1 unique-person result was found in the searched primary sources. The 30.4m number remains an unverified secondary report at this cutoff.
3. No located primary source substantiates “26.4m annual unique bettors.” The official release found says 25.2m. Keep the 26.4m claim out until its original source, period, universe and deduplication method are identified.
4. DataSenado's 20.34m spend-bracket total versus 22.13m press summary is unresolved. The former is a sum of weighted bracket estimates; the latter is a 13% headline. No reason for the difference is asserted here.
5. ANBIMA's national survey, SPA's regulated-platform reporting and DataSenado's prior-30-day probability survey have different universes, windows, methods and definitions. Do not add their counts or use them as a single “market audience.”
6. No bettor-specific individual income, willingness to pay, demand for BETTEX, or conversion estimate is established. The ANBIMA R$5,402 result is survey-reported average household income for bettors only.
