## Primary sources

### Securities and Investment Business Act, Revised Edition 2020
- Title/date: *Securities and Investment Business Act*, Revised Edition, revision date 1 January 2020 (official BVI FSC PDF).
- URL: https://www.bvifsc.vg/sites/default/files/securities_and_investment_business_act.pdf
- Research copy: `research/sources/bvi-gap-law-07.md`
- SIBA s.3: “A person carries on investment business if, by way of business, he or she engages in an activity that—(a) is of a kind specified as an investment activity in Schedule 2, Part A; and (b) is not excluded by Schedule 2, Part B.”
- SIBA s.4(1)-(3): prohibits carrying on (or holding out as carrying on) investment business in/from the BVI without a licence for that kind of business, subject to statutory exceptions and Schedule 2 Part C exclusions; a BVI business company doing the activity outside the BVI is deemed to carry on business from within the BVI.
- Schedule 2, Part A, para. 1(b): principal dealing is included where the person holds itself out as continuously willing to transact at its own prices, underwrites, holds itself out as a market maker/dealer buying for resale, or regularly solicits members of the public to transact and the transaction results from that solicitation.
- Schedule 2, Part B, para. 1(2): “The issuing, redeeming or re-purchasing by a company of its own—(i) shares; (ii) debentures; or (iii) instruments giving entitlement to shares in, or debentures issued by, the company.” “Debenture” includes any other instrument falling within Schedule 1 para. 2. Separate clauses cover a unit trust’s own fund interests and a partnership’s own partnership interests.
- Schedule 1, para. 2: debt instruments include “any other instruments creating or acknowledging indebtedness,” subject to listed exclusions.
- SIBA s.63A: private investment fund means a company, partnership, unit trust or other body which “(a) collects and pools investor funds for the purpose of collective investment and diversification of portfolio risk; and (b) issues fund interests, which entitle the holder to receive an amount computed by reference to the value of a proportionate interest in the whole or in a part of the net assets” of the entity. No “on demand” or redemption requirement appears in this private-investment-fund definition.
- SIBA s.63B(1)-(2): subject to statutory exceptions, a private investment fund may not carry on/hold itself out as such in or from the BVI unless recognized or specified by Commission order. Section 63B(4) deems a BVI business company/private entity organized under BVI law to carry on business from within BVI even if it operates outside.
- Caveat: this is the 2020 Revised Edition, so the 2023 Act and 2024 Order must be read alongside it.

## Scope and interpretive limits

- BETTEX’s stated payout formula—`max(0,min(R,P-B))`, where `P` is updated treasury net assets including open positions—does put a cap by reference to a net-asset measure (`P-B`). That is a genuine textual/factual link to assets and makes “no NAV reference” an overstatement. However, the statutory wording requires a “proportionate interest” in all or part of net assets; a cap on cumulative realized profit by treasury NAV less a fixed/adjusted base does not itself establish that holders own such a proportionate interest. The classification is interpretive and unresolved on this evidence.
- No shares, voting rights, treasury ownership, or principal redemption promise do not answer whether the token is a “fund interest,” debt security/debenture, or other investment. A holder’s enforceable USDC claim arising after calculation supports characterization as a contractual claim; its precise legal form and entitlement formula matter.
- The own-issue exclusion is narrow and concerns the issuer’s own shares, debentures, or entitlements to either, and only the listed issuing/redemption/repurchase activity. A contingent profit debt could potentially be a debenture if it creates/acknowledges indebtedness, but whether these token terms do so—and whether the exclusion reaches the actual activity—is unresolved. It does not exempt a vehicle from the separate s.63A fund definition.
- Dealing in investments “by way of business” must first meet Schedule 2 Part A. Own-account principal trading is not a blanket exemption; conversely, principal dealing is not automatically captured absent the listed holding-out, underwriting, market-maker/dealer, or solicitation conditions. Trading treasury through brokers alone settles neither point.
- One BVI company, retail status, 100,000 holders, and no licence/approval do not independently determine whether s.63A applies. The s.63A definition itself states no investor-count or professional-investor threshold; any investor limits elsewhere are a separate recognition/conduct issue, not a general definition exclusion.
