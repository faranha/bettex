## Source quality assessment and legal cross-check notes

- **Highest authority:** the NBS-hosted English LDA text fetched to `tokens-01.md` (RS Official Gazette No. 153/2020), the NBS rules index fetched to `tokens-02.md`, and the Commission licensing notice fetched to `tokens-03.md`. The English Act is an official regulator-hosted translation but identifies itself as the 2020 Act; it is not a demonstrated consolidated Serbian-language version current through 2026.
- **Allocation of supervision:** LDA Article 10 assigns virtual-currency matters to NBS and digital-token / financial-instrument matters to the Securities Commission; hybrid assets may engage both. Classification is substance-based and cannot be settled by naming BETTEX a token, DAO, fund, or AI product.
- **Article 7 is not a general EUR 3m fundraising exemption:** it exempts a qualifying non-share-like, non-share-fungible financial-instrument digital asset from Capital Market Law treatment only if that issuer's issuance during a 12-month period does not exceed EUR 3m equivalent. It does not waive LDA white-paper rules, service-provider licensing, AML, or other regimes. The fetched Act's LDA Article 17 separately lists narrow no-approved-white-paper advertising cases: fewer than 20 addressed persons; at most 20 tokens issued; per-buyer investment at least EUR 50,000 equivalent; or issuer aggregate issuance no more than EUR 100,000 equivalent in 12 months. Those are alternatives, not the Article 7 EUR 3m test.
- **BETTEX classification and issuance:** a transferable token that gives holders an entitlement to a proportion of treasury profits has a plausible investment/property-right character under LDA Article 2(3), and may have financial-instrument features; the precise rights, governance, transferability, redemption, pooling, and marketing are unknown. Commission jurisdiction under Article 10 is likely for a digital token; if it also constitutes a financial instrument, Article 7 and capital-market rules need separate analysis. Do not state definitive securities classification without regulator-level review.
- **White paper:** the basic rule is approval before advertising an initial offering (LDA Article 17); the full Act's white-paper approval provisions (Article 19) and contents (Article 20) require disclosure of token rights, use of proceeds, technical design, safeguards, risks, AML/CFT procedures, and applicable law. A proposed billion-token total supply or “65% public sale” does not itself prove any exemption. The actual issue/offer amount and manner matter.
- **Own licence vs third party:** LDA Article 4 requires a licence for a legal person providing one or more Article 3 services; Article 3 includes custody/administration where control of access means (e.g. keys) is exercised, placing/issuance services, transfer services, and portfolio management. A user-exportable personal wallet may support an argument that BETTEX does not custody that user's key if BETTEX truly has no control, but does not address control of pooled treasury USDC, app-side staking, order execution, or a delegated investment mandate. The statute's portfolio-management category expressly means managing *individual* digital-asset portfolios under user mandates and a special contract (Article 3); it must not be casually equated with BETTEX's collective pool. Conversely, the individual-portfolio definition does not establish that collective management is unregulated: investment-fund, securities, payments, gambling, and other regimes are outside this limited digital-assets research.
- **Capital:** LDA Article 54 provides EUR 20,000 equivalent for Article 3(1) services 1–6 and EUR 50,000 equivalent for services 7–8. Stojković Attorneys' search snippet reports EUR 125,000 for operating a digital-asset trading platform (service 9), with EUR 20,000 for a platform limited to tokens of a single issuer. The NBS-hosted English PDF's extracted Article 54 text omits the subparagraph (1)(3) amount, although the next paragraph refers to item 3; treat EUR 125,000 as a plausible and corroborated statutory figure, but confirm from current Serbian-language law and regulator rules. At least half of minimum capital is subscribed and paid in money, and capital must be maintained. These are provider minima, not a complete 2026 all-in budget.
- **Outsourcing:** NBS's 2022 paper asks licensing applicants to describe planned operational outsourcing. That supports disclosure and review, not a conclusion that hiring a licensed custodian or manager automatically removes BETTEX's own authorization duties. Contracting, actual control, customer interface, and the regulated provider's permitted scope need fact-specific confirmation.
- **AML:** the LDA's general obligations are not optional because the product is decentralized or uses AI. Fetched Act text points to Article 74 (service-provider compliance with AML/CFT rules) and Article 84 (retention of user identity/AML-required information). The prompt's “AML Article 16” likely refers to a provision in a separate AML statute, not LDA Article 16; the exact current consolidated AML article and applicable measures were not validated in fetched sources. Treat user onboarding without a BETTEX-run KYC flow as an unresolved design/compliance issue, not as an established exemption.
- **2024–2026 version check:** APML search results establish AML/CFT amendments entered into force 14 March 2025, but this research did not retrieve the amended consolidated text or verify the exact customer-identification article and changes relevant to crypto providers. It did not establish whether amendments to the LDA or relevant Serbian Capital Markets Act took effect in 2024–2026, or whether the English 2020 LDA PDF is updated. NBS's digital-assets regulations index lists the Act as Official Gazette No. 153/2020 and several instruments, including 2021 licensing/capital/white-paper decisions and 2023 reporting rules; this is not affirmative proof that there were no later amendments. Check Serbian-language gazette and regulator rules before treating numbers and articles as current on 2026-10-02.
