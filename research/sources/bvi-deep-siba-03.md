### Jurisdiction chapters

**1. Regulatory Framework**

1.1**What is the key legislation that governs the establishment and operation of Alternative Investment Funds?**

The principal legislation governing the establishment and operation of alternative investment funds (“AIFs”) in the British Virgin Islands (“BVI”) is the Securities and Investment Business Act, Revised Edition 2020 (“SIBA”). SIBA provides the overarching regulatory framework for funds, fund managers and other market participants. Key subordinate legislation includes the Public Funds Code, Revised Edition 2020, the Mutual Funds Regulations, Revised Edition 2020, the Private Investment Funds Regulations, Revised Edition 2020 and the Securities and Investment Business (Incubator and Approved Funds) Regulations, Revised Edition 2020.

The BVI Financial Services Commission (“FSC”) is the primary regulatory authority responsible for the registration, supervision, and oversight of investment funds and fund managers. The BVI Business Companies Act, Revised Edition 2020 (“BCA”) governs the corporate formation of fund vehicles, while the Limited Partnership Act, Revised Edition 2020 (“LPA”) governs Limited Partnership structures. The Segregated Portfolio Companies (BVI Business Company) Regulations, Revised Edition 2020 (“SPC Regulations”) is also relevant for umbrella structures.

1.2**Are managers to Alternative Investment Funds required to be licensed, authorised or regulated by a regulatory body?**

Yes. Under SIBA, any person carrying on “investment business” in or from the BVI must hold an investment business licence issued by the FSC. Investment business includes managing investments belonging to another person. Fund managers are therefore required to obtain a Category 3 (Investment Management) licence (sub-category B, managing mutual funds) from the FSC unless they fall within a recognised exemption (for example, where the manager is located outside the BVI and does not carry on business within the jurisdiction). Alternatively, the BVI has a light-touch regulatory framework under which qualifying investment managers may register as approved managers. Approved managers are subject to less stringent regulatory requirements; however, aggregate caps apply with respect to assets under management. Approved managers are subject to caps of USD400 million aggregate assets under management for open-ended structures and USD1 billion of aggregate capital commitments for closed-ended structures.

1.3**Are Alternative Investment Funds themselves required to be licensed, authorised or regulated by a regulatory body?**

Yes. AIFs domiciled in the BVI must be recognised or registered with the FSC under SIBA. The applicable recognition or registration category depends on the nature of the fund:

- Public funds require full recognition under Part II of SIBA and must comply with the Public Funds Code.
- Private placement funds must be registered if they have more than one investor. The Private Investment Funds Regulations govern closed-ended funds whilst traditional open-ended funds are registered under the Mutual Funds Regulations. Mutuals funds are divided into two categories:
  - private funds, which are either limited to (i) invitations made on a private basis, or (ii) no more than 50 investors; and
  - professional funds, which are only available to professional investors.

The Securities and Investment Business (Incubator and Approved Funds) Regulations provides for the registration of open-ended small-scale funds such as:

- Approved funds (which are subject to meeting eligibility criteria, including a maximum of 20 investors and maximum net assets of USD100 million).
- Incubator funds (which are subject to meeting eligibility criteria, a maximum of 20 investors and maximum net assets of USD20 million).

1.4**Does the regulatory regime distinguish between open-ended and closed-ended Alternative Investment Funds (or otherwise differentiate between different types of funds or strategies (e.g. private equity vs hedge funds))?**

SIBA and its subsidiary regulations do not impose strategy-specific restrictions (e.g., between private equity and hedge fund strategies). However, the regulatory regime does distinguish between open-ended funds (which offer redemption rights to investors) and closed-ended funds (which do not). This distinction is relevant primarily for determining the applicable registration category and ongoing regulatory obligations. The FSC does not prescribe different rules based on investment strategy.

1.5**What does the authorisation process involve for managers and, if applicable, Alternative Investment Funds, and how long does the process typically take?**

For fund managers, the application for an investment business licence is submitted to the FSC and must include details of the applicant’s directors, shareholders, compliance arrangements, business plan, and evidence of adequate resources. The FSC typically processes licence applications within two to four weeks, although this may vary depending on the complexity of the application and the completeness of supporting documentation.

For funds, the registration process for private placement funds involves submission of a prescribed application form, the fund’s constitutional documents and the offering document to the FSC. Registration of private placement funds is typically completed within five to seven business days of a complete application being filed. The timeframe varies by product: incubator and approved funds may commence business within approximately two business days of filing a complete application; a professional fund may launch and apply to the FSC within 14 days of commencing business; whereas a private fund must be recognised by the FSC before it launches. Public fund recognition involves a more detailed review and may take several weeks.

1.6**Are there local residence or other local qualification or substance requirements for managers and/or Alternative Investment Funds?**

There is no statutory requirement for a fund manager to be resident in the BVI. However, if a manager wishes to hold an investment business licence issued by the FSC, it must be incorporated or registered in the BVI and must have at least two directors who are fit and proper persons.

Fund management business is one of the relevant activities identified in the Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act, Revised Edition 2020 (“ES Act”) and managers that hold a Category 3 (Investment Management) licence must satisfy the BVI economic substance test. The test generally requires that the relevant activity be directed and managed in the BVI, that the entity has adequate qualified employees, expenditure and premises in the BVI having regard to the nature and scale of the activity, and that its core income-generating activities be conducted in the BVI.

A fund is not required to meet economic substance requirements unless it carries out one of the relevant activities outlined in the ES Act. Whilst there is no requirement for a fund to maintain a physical office in the BVI, a fund must appoint a BVI-licensed registered agent and maintain a registered office in the jurisdiction.

1.7**Are any service providers specifically required to be appointed in respect of Alternative Investment Funds?**

Yes. All BVI funds must appoint a BVI-licensed registered agent and an authorised representative. Public funds must also appoint an auditor, a custodian, a manager and an administrator. All private placement funds save for approved and incubator funds must appoint an auditor (unless an exemption applies) and a custodian or prime broker to hold fund assets (with certain exceptions). The appointment of an administrator is not mandatory for incubator funds.

1.8**What rules apply to foreign managers wishing to manage funds domiciled in your jurisdiction?**

Foreign managers are not required to obtain a BVI investment business licence solely by reason of managing a BVI-domiciled fund, provided they do not carry on investment business “in or from” the BVI. In practice, most BVI funds are managed by offshore or onshore managers based outside the BVI (e.g., in London, New York, Hong Kong or the Cayman Islands). The fund’s offering document will typically disclose the manager’s regulatory status in its home jurisdiction.

Where a foreign manager appoints a BVI-licensed sub-manager or establishes a BVI subsidiary, the local entity will require a licence from the FSC.

1.9**Are advisers (including foreign advisers) to Alternative Investment Funds required to be licensed, authorised or regulated by a regulatory body?**

Under SIBA, persons providing “investment advice” in or from the BVI are required to hold an investment business licence (Category 3 – Investment Advising). However, foreign-based advisers who do not carry on business in or from the BVI are not subject to BVI licensing requirements. In practice, most investment advisers to BVI funds are regulated in their home jurisdiction and do not require a separate BVI licence.

**2. Fund Structures**

2.1**What are the principal legal structures used for Alternative Investment Funds (including reference where relevant to local asset holding companies)?**

The most commonly used legal structures for AIFs in the BVI are:

1. BVI Business Companies – incorporated under the BCA. These are the most popular vehicle for open-ended hedge funds. Investors subscribe for redeemable shares.
2. Segregated Portfolio Companies (“SPCs”) – formed in accordance with the BCA and the SPC Regulations. Allows a single legal entity to maintain segregated portfolios with legally ring-fenced assets and liabilities.
3. Limited Partnerships – established under the LPA. These are the preferred structure for closed-ended private equity and venture capital funds.
4. Unit Trusts – established by trust deed under general BVI trust law. These are less common but occasionally used for specific investor bases.

Local asset holding companies are typically BVI Business Companies used as special purpose vehicles within a fund’s investment structure.

2.2**Do any of the legal structures operate as an umbrella structure with several sub-funds, and if yes, is segregation of assets between the sub-funds a legally recognised feature of the structure?**

Yes. SPCs may operate as umbrella structures with multiple segregated portfolios. Segregation of assets and liabilities between portfolios is a legally recognised and statutorily protected feature under the Segregated Portfolio Companies Act, 2018. Each segregated portfolio is treated as a separate pool of assets, and creditors of one portfolio have no recourse to the assets of another portfolio.

BVI Business Companies may also establish multi-class share structures to create economic segregation between different series or strategies, although this does not provide the same statutory ring-fencing as an SPC.

2.3**Please describe the limited liability of investors in respect of the different legal structures used for Alternative Investment Funds.**

In a BVI Business Company or SPC, shareholders’ liability is limited to the amount unpaid (if any) on their shares. In a Limited Partnership, limited partners’ liability is limited to the amount of their capital contribution, provided they do not take part in the management of the partnership. Unit Trust investors’ liability is typically limited to the value of their units by the terms of the trust deed.

2.4**What are the principal legal structures used for managers and advisers of Alternative Investment Funds?**

Fund managers and advisers are most commonly structured as BVI Business Companies or as entities incorporated in other jurisdictions (e.g., Cayman Islands exempted companies, UK limited companies or Delaware LLCs). Where a BVI-licensed manager is required, it will be a BVI Business Company holding an investment business licence from the FSC.

2.5**Are there any limits on the manager’s ability to restrict redemptions in open-ended funds or transfers in open-ended or closed-ended funds?**

There are no statutory restrictions on a manager’s ability to impose gates, lock-ups or suspension of redemptions in open-ended funds. These matters are governed by the fund’s constitutional documents and offering terms. BVI law affords significant contractual freedom to structure redemption terms, including the imposition of redemption gates, side pockets and in-kind redemptions.

2.6**Are there any legislative restrictions on transfers of investors’ interests in Alternative Investment Funds?**

There are no specific legislative restrictions on the transfer of investors’ interests in AIFs. Transfers are governed by the constitutional documents of the fund and may be subject to anti-money laundering and beneficial ownership reporting requirements.

2.7**Are there any other limitations on a manager’s ability to manage its funds (e.g. diversification requirements, asset stripping rules)?**

There are no statutory diversification requirements or asset stripping rules imposed on BVI AIFs. Fund managers have broad investment discretion, subject to the investment strategy and restrictions set out in the fund’s offering document and constitutional documents. The FSC does not impose prescriptive investment guidelines on private placement funds.

2.8**Does the fund remunerate investment managers through management fee or performance fee/carried interest or by a combination of management fee and carried interest? In the case of carried interest, how is this typically structured?**

BVI AIFs typically remunerate managers through a combination of management fees and performance fees (for open-ended funds) or carried interest (for closed-ended funds). Management fees are usually charged as a percentage of net asset value (commonly 1.5–2% _per annum_). Performance fees are typically 15–20% of net profits, subject to high-water marks.

For closed-ended fund structures, carried interest is typically structured as a profit allocation to the general partner (or an affiliated carried interest vehicle) equal to 20% of net profits, subject to a preferred return hurdle (commonly 8% _per annum_) and a catch-up mechanism.

**3. Marketing**

3.1**What is the key legislation that governs the production and use of marketing materials?**

The production and distribution of marketing materials for BVI funds is primarily governed by SIBA and guidance issued by the FSC. There is no separate BVI prospectus or marketing-specific legislation. The content of offering documents for public funds must comply with the Public Funds Code, which prescribes minimum disclosure requirements.

For private placement funds, marketing is subject to the general prohibition on making offers to the public. Marketing materials must not be misleading and must be consistent with the fund’s offering document.

3.2**What are the key content requirements for marketing materials, whether due to legal requirements or customary practice?**

There are no prescriptive content requirements for marketing materials of private placement funds beyond the general prohibition on misleading statements. As a matter of customary practice, marketing materials typically include risk warnings, a description of the investment strategy, fees and a disclaimer that the material does not constitute an offer to the public.

Public fund offering documents must comply with the disclosure requirements of the Public Funds Code, including details of the fund’s investment objectives, risks, fees, service providers and governance arrangements.

3.3**Do marketing documents need to be registered with or approved by the local regulator?**

No. Marketing documents for private placement funds are not required to be filed with or approved by the FSC. However, offering documents are filed with the FSC as part of the fund registration process. Public fund prospectuses require FSC approval.

3.4**What restrictions (and, if applicable, ongoing regulatory requirements) are there on marketing Alternative Investment Funds?**

Funds that do not meet the requirements of a public fund under SIBA may only be marketed by way of private placement and may not make offers to the public. There is no statutory definition of “private placement” in BVI law, and market practice is guided by the exemptions and safe harbours available under the securities laws of the jurisdictions in which the fund is being marketed (e.g., Regulation D in the US and the UK FCA’s financial promotion regime).

There are no ongoing BVI-specific regulatory requirements with respect to marketing, beyond the obligation to ensure materials are not misleading.

3.5**Is the concept of “pre-marketing” (or equivalent) recognised in your jurisdiction? If so, how has it been defined (by law and/or practice)?**

The concept of “pre-marketing” is not formally defined or regulated under BVI law. In practice, managers routinely engage in pre-marketing activities (such as gauging investor interest before a fund launch) without triggering regulatory obligations in the BVI. The regulatory position in the investors’ home jurisdictions will, however, be relevant.

3.6**Can Alternative Investment Funds be marketed to retail investors (including any specific treatment for high-net-worth individuals or semi-professional or similar categories)?**

Public funds recognised under SIBA may be marketed to retail investors. All other regulated funds are restricted to private placement and are therefore not available to retail investors. The BVI does not have a formal statutory category of “semi-professional” or “high-net-worth” investor.

Only professional investors are qualified to invest in professional funds. A professional investor must be either (i) a person whose ordinary business entails the acquisition or disposal of the property of the same kind that are held by the fund in question, or (ii) a person who has signed a declaration confirming that, either individually or jointly with his or her spouse, he or she has a net worth exceeding USD1 million (or its equivalent).

3.7**Do any additional regimes need to be considered where investors are retail investors?**

Where a fund is structured as a public fund and marketed to retail investors, the Public Funds Code imposes additional requirements regarding disclosure, governance and the appointment of service providers (including a custodian and administrator). There are no additional BVI consumer protection or retail investor regimes beyond the Public Funds Code.

3.8**What qualification requirements must be met in relation to prospective investors?**

Only professional investors may invest in professional funds. Unless exempted by statute, the minimum initial investment in a professional fund is USD100,000 (or its equivalent).

For incubator funds, the initial investment for each investor must be at least USD20,000 (or its equivalent).

A fund’s offering document may impose additional qualification requirements (e.g., “accredited investor” or “qualified purchaser” standards) to facilitate compliance with securities laws in the jurisdictions where the fund is marketed.

3.9**Are there additional restrictions on marketing to public bodies such as government pension funds?**

There are no BVI-specific restrictions on marketing to public bodies or government pension funds. However, managers should be mindful of the regulatory requirements and internal investment policies applicable to such investors in their home jurisdictions.

3.10**Are there any restrictions on the participation in Alternative Investment Funds by particular types of investors (whether as sponsors or investors)?**

There are no BVI-specific restrictions on the types of investors that may participate in AIFs. The fund’s constitutional documents and offering terms will typically specify eligibility criteria based on the securities laws of the jurisdictions in which the fund is marketed.

3.11**Are there any restrictions on the use of intermediaries to assist in the fundraising process?**

There are no BVI-specific restrictions on the use of placement agents or intermediaries. However, intermediaries marketing fund interests in or from the BVI may need to hold an investment business licence if their activities constitute “investment business” under SIBA. Intermediaries operating outside the BVI will be subject to the regulatory requirements of their home jurisdictions.

**4. Investments**

4.1**Are there any restrictions on the types of investment activities that can be performed by Alternative Investment Funds?**

There are no statutory restrictions on the types of investment activities that may be performed by BVI AIFs. Funds are free to pursue any investment strategy, subject to the terms of their offering documents and constitutional documents.

4.2**Are there any limitations on the types of investments that can be included in an Alternative Investment Fund’s portfolio, whether for diversification reasons or otherwise?**

No. The BVI regulatory regime does not impose diversification requirements or restrictions on the types of assets that may be held by private placement funds. Public funds may be subject to certain restrictions under the Public Funds Code.

4.3**Are there any local regulatory requirements that apply to investing in particular investments (e.g. derivatives or loans)?**

There are no BVI-specific regulatory requirements that apply to particular asset classes such as derivatives or loans. BVI AIFs may invest in any asset class without restriction, subject to the terms of their offering documents.

4.4**Are there any restrictions on borrowing by the Alternative Investment Fund?**

There are no statutory restrictions on borrowing by BVI AIFs. Leverage limits, if any, are set by the terms of the fund’s offering document and constitutional documents.

4.5**Are there are any restrictions on who holds the Alternative Investment Fund’s assets?**

Private placement funds, save for approved and incubator funds, must appoint a custodian to hold fund assets unless an exemption applies (for example, where the fund invests in assets that cannot practicably be held by a custodian, such as real estate or private equity interests). Approved and incubator funds are not required to appoint a custodian; however, they must maintain appropriate safekeeping arrangements; prime brokerage arrangements are commonly used.

**5. Disclosure of Information**

5.1**What disclosure must the Alternative Investment Fund or its manager make to prospective investors, investors, regulators or other parties, including on environmental, social and/or governance factors?**

Disclosure to prospective investors is made through the fund’s offering document, which sets out the fund’s investment strategy, risks, fees, governance, conflicts of interest and terms of participation. There is no BVI-specific statutory requirement to make ESG-related disclosures, although market practice increasingly includes ESG-related information in offering documents where relevant to the fund’s strategy.

Funds must file their offering documents with the FSC. The FSC may request additional information as part of its supervisory activities.

5.2**Are there any requirements to provide details of participants (whether owners, controllers or investors) in Alternative Investment Funds or managers established in your jurisdiction (including details of investors) to any local regulator or record-keeping agency, e.g., for the purposes of a public (or non-public) register of beneficial owners?**

Yes. The beneficial ownership regime is now contained in the BCA and the BVI Business Companies and Limited Partnerships (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations, 2024 (as amended). Since 2 January 2025, beneficial ownership information has been filed with the BVI Registrar of Corporate Affairs (“Registrar”) through the online VIRRGIN system, and filings under the former Beneficial Ownership Secure Search System Act have ended. A BVI company or Limited Partnership must collect, keep and maintain adequate, accurate and up-to-date information on its beneficial owners and, unless an exemption applies, file that information with the Registrar within 30 days of incorporation, registration or continuation, updating it within 30 days of any change. Regulated funds benefit from an exemption from the filing obligation: a company that is a private, professional, public, private investment, incubator or approved fund is exempt from filing beneficial ownership information, provided the information can be supplied to the Registrar within 24 hours of a request by a person holding a Category 6 licence, the fund’s authorised representative or another person licensed by the FSC with a physical presence in the BVI. The same fund categories are exempt from the new requirement for companies to file a copy of their register of members with the Registrar. The BVI does not operate a public register of beneficial ownership. Since 1 April 2026, a legitimate-interest access regime has been in force, under which a person may apply to inspect the register where they can demonstrate a legitimate interest connected to the investigation, prevention or detection of money laundering, terrorist financing or proliferation financing, or where they are an obliged entity carrying out customer due diligence. Access is confined to beneficial owners holding 25% or more of the ownership interests or control, and the regime includes safeguards, including notice to the entity, a five-day window to object and a separate process (open since 2 January 2026) by which a beneficial owner may apply for an exemption from disclosure.

5.3**What are the reporting requirements to investors or regulators in relation to Alternative Investment Funds or their managers, including on environmental, social and/or governance factors?**

Private placement funds must file annual returns with the FSC confirming compliance with applicable regulations. All private placement funds, save for approved and incubator funds, are required to prepare annual audited financial statements and make the statements available to investors. There are no BVI-specific ESG reporting requirements for fund managers or funds, although managers subject to regulation in other jurisdictions (e.g., under the EU SFDR or UK SDR) may be required to make ESG-related disclosures.

Reporting to investors is governed by the terms of the offering document and constitutional documents, and typically includes periodic net asset value statements, annual audited financial statements and investor letters.

5.4**Is the use of side letters restricted?**

No. There are no BVI-specific restrictions on the use of side letters. Side letters are commonly used to grant preferential terms to certain investors (e.g., fee discounts, enhanced reporting, co-investment rights or most-favoured-nation provisions). The fund’s offering document typically discloses the possibility of side letter arrangements.

**6. Taxation**

6.1**What is the tax treatment of the principal forms of Alternative Investment Funds and local asset holding companies identified in question 2.1?**

The BVI does not impose income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax or any other form of direct taxation on companies, partnerships or trusts. Accordingly, BVI AIFs (whether structured as companies, SPCs, Limited Partnerships or Unit Trusts) and BVI asset holding companies are not subject to BVI tax on their income, gains or distributions.

There is no BVI value-added tax, sales tax or estate duty applicable to fund operations.

6.2**What is the tax treatment of the principal forms of investment manager/adviser identified in question 2.4?**

BVI-incorporated or registered managers and advisers are similarly not subject to income tax, capital gains tax or withholding tax in the BVI. Management fees, performance fees and carried interest received by BVI managers are not subject to BVI taxation.

6.3**Are there any establishment or transfer taxes levied in connection with an investor’s participation in an Alternative Investment Fund or the transfer of the investor’s interest?**

No. There is no stamp duty, transfer tax or establishment tax levied in the BVI in connection with the subscription for, or transfer of, interests in a BVI AIF.

6.4**What is the local tax treatment of (a) resident, (b) non-resident, and (c) pension fund investors (or any other common investor type) in Alternative Investment Funds?**

All categories of investor – whether BVI-resident, non-resident or pension funds – are treated equally for BVI tax purposes. No BVI tax is imposed on dividends, interest or capital gains received by investors from BVI AIFs.

6.5**Is it necessary or advisable to obtain a tax ruling from the tax or regulatory authorities prior to establishing an Alternative Investment Fund or local asset holding company?**

No. Given the absence of direct taxation in the BVI, it is neither necessary nor customary to obtain a tax ruling prior to establishing a fund or asset holding company.

6.6**What steps have been taken to implement the US Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act 2010 (FATCA) and other similar information reporting regimes such as the OECD’s Common Reporting Standard?**

The BVI has entered into an intergovernmental agreement with the US for the implementation of FATCA. BVI financial institutions, including funds, are required to identify US reportable accounts and report information to the BVI International Tax Authority, which exchanges information with the US Internal Revenue Service.

The BVI has also implemented the OECD Common Reporting Standard (“CRS”) through the Mutual Legal Assistance (Tax Matters) Act, Revised Edition 2020 and related guidance. BVI funds are required to identify and report on account holders who are tax resident in CRS-participating jurisdictions.[1](https://iclg.com/practice-areas/alternative-investment-funds-laws-and-regulations/british-virgin-islands#endnote-1)

6.7**What steps have been or are being taken to implement the OECD’s Action Plan on Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS), in particular Actions 2 (hybrids/reverse hybrids/shell entities) (e.g. ATAD I and II), 6 (prevention of treaty abuse) (e.g. the MLI), and 7 (permanent establishments), insofar as they affect Alternative Investment Funds’ and local asset holding companies’ operations?**

The BVI has committed to implementing the BEPS minimum standards and has signed the Multilateral Convention to Implement Tax Treaty Related Measures to Prevent BEPS (“MLI”). The BVI’s network of tax information exchange agreements (rather than comprehensive double taxation agreements) means that the practical impact of the MLI on BVI funds is limited.

The BVI introduced economic substance requirements through the ES Act, which requires BVI entities carrying on certain “relevant activities” (including fund management, holding business and intellectual property business) to demonstrate adequate economic substance in the BVI. Funds themselves are generally excluded from economic substance requirements where they are regulated under SIBA; however, they are required to file an annual economic substance declaration within six months of the end of their financial period.

6.8**What steps have been or are being taken to implement the OECD’s Global Anti-Base Erosion (GloBE) rules, insofar as they affect Alternative Investment Funds’ and local asset holding companies’ operations? Do the domestic rules depart significantly from the OECD’s model rules, insofar as they affect Alternative Investment Funds’ and local asset holding companies’ operations?**

As at the date of writing, the BVI has not enacted domestic legislation implementing the OECD’s Pillar Two GloBE rules. The BVI does not levy corporate income tax and has not introduced a qualifying domestic minimum top-up tax. BVI funds and holding companies may, however, be affected by GloBE rules enacted in other jurisdictions (e.g., where a multinational group’s ultimate parent entity is located in a jurisdiction that has adopted an Income Inclusion Rule). Managers should monitor developments in this area closely.

6.9**Are there any tax-advantaged asset classes or structures available? How widely are they deployed?**

Given the tax-neutral status of the BVI, there is no concept of “tax-advantaged” asset classes or structures within the jurisdiction itself. The BVI’s broad tax neutrality is the principal advantage, and this applies equally to all asset classes and structures.

6.10**Are there any other material tax issues for investors, managers, advisers or Alternative Investment Funds?**

The principal tax consideration for investors is their own tax position in their home jurisdiction. BVI funds are commonly used as tax-neutral pooling vehicles, and investors are generally subject to tax in their country of residence on their share of fund income or gains. Investors and managers should consider the application of controlled foreign company rules, anti-avoidance provisions and reporting obligations in their home jurisdictions.

6.11**Are there any meaningful tax changes anticipated in the coming 12 months other than as set out at question 6.6 above?**

No significant changes to the BVI tax regime are anticipated in the year ahead. The BVI government continues to monitor international developments, including the OECD Pillar Two rules, but has not signalled an intention to introduce direct taxation. The BVI International Tax Authority continues to refine its CRS and FATCA guidance in line with evolving international standards.

**7. Trends and Reforms**

7.1**What have been the main trends in the Alternative Investment Funds space in the last 12 months?**

Key trends in the BVI fund space over the past 12 months include:

1. Continued strong demand for BVI Limited Partnership structures for private equity, venture capital and credit funds, driven by the flexibility and efficiency of the LPA.
2. Increased focus on regulatory compliance, particularly in light of ongoing international scrutiny of offshore financial centres and the BVI’s commitment to meeting OECD and EU standards.
3. Growing use of SPCs as umbrella platforms for multi-strategy managers and fund-of-funds structures, reflecting investor demand for operational efficiency and segregated liability.
4. Heightened attention to ESG considerations, with managers increasingly integrating ESG factors into fund documentation and reporting to meet investor expectations and regulatory requirements in their home jurisdictions.
5. Continued digitalisation of fund administration and regulatory filings, including the FSC’s ongoing enhancement of its electronic filing systems.

7.2**In your opinion, what reforms (if any) in the Alternative Investment Funds space would be advantageous for the evolution of the private markets?**

Further reforms that would support the continued evolution of the BVI as a leading fund domicile include:

1. Introduction of a statutory framework for digital assets funds, providing legal certainty for tokenised fund interests and digital asset investment strategies.
2. Continued enhancement of the BVI’s beneficial ownership regime to maintain alignment with international standards while preserving the efficiency and confidentiality valued by investors.
3. Streamlining of the fund registration process through further digitalisation and the introduction of expedited review tracks for experienced managers.
4. Development of guidance on ESG disclosure and reporting tailored to BVI funds, to assist managers in meeting evolving investor and regulatory expectations without imposing disproportionate compliance burdens.
5. Continued engagement with international standard-setting bodies (including the OECD and FATF) to ensure that the BVI regulatory framework remains fit for purpose and internationally competitive.
