## 6. Current gambling statute (official English PDF result)
- Title: Law on Games of Chance (official English PDF; Gazette RS 18/2020, 94/2024)
- URL: https://www.uis.gov.rs/static/uploads/2753_Law_on_games_of_chance.pdf
- Date: Current consolidation identifies 2020 statute and 2024 amendment; search checked 2 October 2026.
- Tier: 1 (official Games of Chance Administration PDF)
- Saved path: Search result only; current consolidated Serbian text fetched and saved as research/sources/entity-01.md.
- Snippet: “LAW ON GAMES OF CHANCE (‘Official Gazette RS’ no. 18/2020, 94/2024)” and Article 1 says it regulates right to organize games of chance/types/conditions/organizer duties/supervision.
- Exact provisions from fetched Serbian editorial consolidation: Articles 1–4 define scope, chance games, and only games listed in the catalogue with rules may be organized; Article 9(1) defines organizer as legal person or entrepreneur acting under Government permit, Administration approval or consent; Article 9(23)–(26) describes age verification, player account, and operator account duties for remote special games; Article 10(3) prohibits participation in foreign-organized games where stakes are paid in Serbia, and Article 10(4) prohibits enabling stakes in Serbia for foreign-organized games; Article 19 transfers the right to organize special machine and betting games by Administration approval; Article 24 says a participant in a classic game is “any person” meeting game conditions; Articles 34–36 include casino organizer conditions and EUR 1 million minimum capital. These do not clearly resolve whether a Serbian DOO may be a bettor on a licensed operator’s platform.

## 11. MiCA third-country token offerors
- Title: Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 (MiCA), EUR-Lex
- URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1114/oj
- Date: 31 May 2023 (adopted); search checked 2 October 2026.
- Tier: 1 (official EU legislation)
- Saved path: Search result only; not fetched.
- Snippet: “Offerors that are established in a third country should notify their crypto-asset white paper and, upon request of the competent authority, their marketing communications, to the competent authority of the Member State where they intend to offer the crypto-assets.”
- Article cross-reference: MiCA Article 15 (third-country offeror notification for relevant crypto-assets); this is not an EU-wide authorization passport. CASP authorization/passport requirements are separate (MiCA Articles 59, 63 and 65; verify applicability and token classification). Serbia is a third country/non-EU; having a Serbian DOO or local permission alone confers no MiCA passport.
